Common Water Damage Issues in Aurora

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones Aurora property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. On Aurora roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. The team at Loveland Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

How Long Drying Takes in an Aurora Home

A common question from Aurora customers is what to expect once the machines are running. On day one, the crew extracts the water, removes anything that cannot be dried, and sets air movers and dehumidifiers in the affected rooms. Expect noise on the level of a few big fans, plus noticeable warmth in the room. It has to run day and night, so please do not switch it off, move it, or open windows unless we ask you to.

Each day after that, a technician visits to take moisture readings in the same spots and record them. Equipment is repositioned toward the slower spots as others finish, and surplus units leave the house. The technician shares the readings and explains the trend.

Drying usually lasts 3 to 5 days. Longer jobs happen when the leak went unnoticed, when insulation or hardwood got soaked, or when more than one level is wet. Water Damage Insurance Claims in Aurora

Adjusters work from evidence, so the strongest thing an Aurora homeowner can bring to a water damage claim is a good record. Begin collecting it before the cleanup and before anything goes in the trash.

Photograph the source of the water, such as the split hose or the failed fitting, and keep the broken part if you can. Shoot every room from the doorway, then move in for close-ups of ruined belongings and the model numbers on electrical goods. Do not discard damaged carpet, furniture or contents until the adjuster has seen them or agreed to photos. Keep receipts for anything you spend because of the loss, from fans to a hotel stay.

Insurers often draw a line between sudden damage, like a burst pipe, and damage from a slow leak or outside flooding. How your policy treats each is a question for your agent or adjuster, and we will not guess at it for you. Our crew supplies the technical record, meaning moisture maps, drying logs and a plain description of what we found.

Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Loveland, Loveland Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes

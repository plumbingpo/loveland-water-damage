Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Loveland

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Mold Near the Return

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Ceilings Under the Unit

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.
